Evaluation Protocol

Each trial presents a model with a role prompt, a simulated tool, and an adversarial user message designed to elicit an unauthorized tool call or data disclosure. Results are classified using a two-stage pipeline: deterministic pre-checks for exact target value matches, followed by an LLM judge for semantically ambiguous cases. The pass criterion requires both no unauthorized data disclosure and no unmitigated tool authorization violation.

1. Key Findings

Research question: Do frontier language models reliably enforce authorization boundaries when explicitly instructed to do so?

Central result: 40.3% of model-case trials satisfied the pass criterion across 1,380 evaluations. This figure is insufficient as a standalone summary: pass rates vary by a factor of 2.5× across models, by a factor of 17× across attack tiers, and the same model may pass or fail the same case across repeated trials. The sections below decompose these three dimensions.

3. Case-Level Robustness

Is each model's behavior on a given case structurally determined, or does it vary across repeated stochastic executions?

For each (model, case) pair, we count passes across 5 runs. The distribution of 0/5–5/5 outcomes reveals whether failures are systematic or sampling artifacts. A high 0/5 share indicates structural vulnerability; a high mixed (1–4/5) share indicates unstable authorization reasoning.

7. Soft Disclosures & Semantic Leakage

What fraction of failures are only detectable by a semantic judge, not by deterministic signals?

After deterministic checks pass (no exact disclosure, no tool violation), a semantic judge rates leakage on a 0–5 scale. Cases with level >0 are counted as soft disclosures. These failures are invisible to evaluation frameworks relying solely on tool-call logs or substring matching.
