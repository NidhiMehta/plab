Silent Judge: Weak LLMs as Evaluators Inflate
Privacy Failure Rates by 3.5× in PLAB
Nidhi Mehta  ·  nidhi_mehta@outlook.com  ·  Independent Researcher
PLAB v0.3 / v0.4  ·  September 2026  ·  github.com/NidhiMehta/plab

We introduce PLAB, a deterministic privacy-attack benchmark for large language models. PLAB v0.3 comprises 3,600 cases spanning 6 domains, 8 attack families, 25 scenario families, and 3 difficulty levels. Evaluating a subject model with a weak LLM judge (qwen3.5:3b) yields 1,441 flagged failures; cross-checking with a stronger judge (claude-opus-4-7) reveals only 412 true failures — a 3.5× inflation. 683 of the original flags (47.4%) are confirmed hallucinations in the weak judge's rationale. PLAB v0.4 introduces 45 hand-crafted cases across four structured tiers with deterministic evaluation for tiers 1–3 and judge-based evaluation for tier 4. Across five frontier models, pass rates range from 88.9% (Gemini 2.5 Pro) to 97.8% (DeepSeek R1). The benchmark, all model outputs, and evaluation code are available at github.com/NidhiMehta/plab.

1  The Judge Inflation Problem

LLM-as-judge evaluation is widely used in privacy benchmarks because exact-match detection misses partial or implicit leakage. However, a judge model can hallucinate a violation where none exists — flagging a model refusal as a leak.

3.5× inflation.
qwen3.5:3b flagged 1,441 / 3,600 cases (40%). A controlled re-evaluation with claude-opus-4-7 confirmed only 412 (11.4%) as true failures. Of the 1,029 disagreements, 683 are confirmed hallucinations (47.4% of all original flags) — the judge invented a violation that was not present.

The remaining 346 disagreements are ambiguous: the case involves borderline partial leakage where reasonable judges differ. We conservatively count these as non-failures in the headline numbers.

3  Controlled Experiment

To isolate judge hallucination from genuine disagreement, we constructed 100 cases where the subject model's response is an unambiguous refusal (ground truth false-positive rate = 0%). We then ran both judges:

JudgeConditionFP rate
qwen3.5:3b — 100%
claude-opus-4-7 Contaminated 0%
claude-opus-4-7 Clean 2%

The weak judge hallucinates violations on every single clear-refusal case. The stronger judge is near-perfect.

4  PLAB v0.4 Tier Design

v0.4 replaces the flat 3,600-case corpus with 45 hand-crafted cases across four structured tiers. Tiers 1–3 use deterministic evaluation; tier 4 uses claude-opus-4-7 as judge.

6  Why Deterministic Evaluation

For tiers 1–3 the violation signal is binary and unambiguous: did the model call a tool it was not authorized to call? Did it output the exact protected string? These checks require no LLM and produce no hallucinations.

Tier 4 (chained attacks) involves multi-turn reasoning where the violation may be implicit across multiple tool calls. A semantic judge is necessary there, but its scope is bounded — a deterministic pre-check gates whether the judge is invoked at all, preventing the class of hallucinations seen in v0.3.

7  Related Work

PrivacyLens (Shao et al., 2024) and CONFAIDE (Mireshghallah et al., 2024) evaluate contextual privacy norms but rely entirely on LLM judges without hallucination auditing. PLAB contributes: (i) a deterministic ground-truth layer that bounds judge influence, (ii) a controlled experiment quantifying judge hallucination rate, and (iii) an explicit anti-pattern taxonomy.
