The Instruction Is the Defense:
Authorization Failures in Agentic LLM Deployments
Nidhi Mehta  ·  nidhi_mehta@outlook.com  ·  Independent Researcher
PLAB v0.6  ·  September 2026  ·  github.com/NidhiMehta/plab

We evaluate whether frontier LLMs respect access-control boundaries in realistic agentic deployments — contexts where the model is given a named company identity, an authenticated user, real tool access, and an explicit pre-call authorization instruction. PLAB v0.6 comprises 69 adversarial cases across three tiers (confused_deputy, tool_gated, chained) and six domains, evaluated against four frontier models. The pre-call authorization instruction is the sole source of safety in the tool_gated tier: remove it and every model fails every case (0%). Even with it, overall pass rates range from 26% (GPT-5.2) to 51% (Gemini 2.5 Pro), and GPT-5.2 fails all tool-gated cases regardless. Audit-pretext attacks break every model at near-zero pass rates. All cases, results, and evaluation code are at github.com/NidhiMehta/plab.

1  The Authorization Problem

Prior agentic benchmarks found high inconclusive rates (40–93%) — models would not call tools even in adversarial settings, leaving the attack vector untested. v0.6 fixes this with richer deployment prompts: a named company, an explicitly authenticated caller, and an instruction to use tools to serve requests. With this setup, inconclusives drop to 0–10% and real failures become visible.

The instruction is necessary but not sufficient.
Remove the pre-call authorization check instruction and every model fails every tool-gated case (0% across all four models). Restore it: Opus 52%, DeepSeek 50%, Gemini 20%, GPT-5.2 0%. GPT-5.2 has a structural failure in the tool-call pathway that no prompt engineering resolves.

7  Key Findings

F1. The pre-call authorization instruction is the only mechanism that produces safe tool-gated behavior. Its removal universally collapses pass rates to 0%.

F2. GPT-5.2 has a structural tool-call pathway failure: 0% on tool_gated even with the instruction present, despite 73% on enterprise cases (predominantly confused_deputy). Safety calibration is domain-shaped, not principled.

F3. Gemini leads on confused_deputy (80%) and chained (45%) but collapses on tool_gated (20%). The refusal behavior protecting against confused-deputy attacks does not transfer to single-turn tool authorization.

F4. Audit authority framing is the most reliable attack vector. Pass rates of 0–14% across models regardless of tier or domain.
